title: How The Concepts Were Chosen
number: 003
summary: The atlas is a loop, not a list — and why narrative is only one station on it.

The central question is how life experiences shape the internal narratives
people carry, and how those narratives shape who they become.

The obvious way to build an archive around that question is to treat it as a
chain: something happens to you, you tell yourself a story about it, the story
becomes your character. Clean, linear, and wrong in a way that would quietly
distort every file in the archive.

## The atlas is a feedback loop

The concepts here were chosen to trace a circuit, not a sequence:

> experience → memory → perception → emotion → learning → narrative →
> identity → beliefs → values → expectations → decisions → behaviour →
> new experience

The last term is the first term. Behaviour produces new experience, which is
encoded by a memory system already shaped by the previous pass, and perceived
through expectations the previous pass installed. Nothing in the loop is
purely upstream of anything else.

This is why the archive has a Connections surface at all. A concept read in
isolation is a concept read wrongly — the interesting behaviour lives in the
coupling between stations, and several of the genuinely open questions in this
archive are questions about the coupling rather than about any single station.

## Narrative is one station, not the mechanism

Narrative earns a place on the loop. It does not get to be the explanation.
Treating self-story as *the* mechanism by which experience becomes identity is
a hypothesis with a literature, not a settled finding, and an archive that
assumed it would be arguing a position while appearing to report one.

So narrative sits where the evidence puts it: one station among several, with
its own file, its own tiers, and its own unknowns about how much work it
actually does.

## What is missing, and why that shows

Not every concept on the loop has been investigated to the same depth. Files
that have been worked through carry full evidence tiers and open questions;
the rest are mapped but not yet filed, and they are displayed that way rather
than padded out to look complete.

A mapped-but-unfiled concept is an honest state for an ongoing investigation.
A template dressed up as a finished file is not.
