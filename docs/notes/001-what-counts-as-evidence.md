title: What Counts As Evidence
number: 001
summary: The citation rule this investigation holds itself to, and what it refuses to display.

Everything asserted as fact anywhere in this archive traces to a real,
published paper. Not a paraphrase of one, not a plausible-sounding study, not
a claim that "research shows." A named paper, with authors, a year, and a
place it was published.

This is a stricter rule than it first appears, and it is the rule the whole
exhibit rests on. An archive built in this aesthetic — case numbers, stamps,
exhibit tags, the visual grammar of authority — could carry an invented
citation a long way before anyone checked. The look of rigour is easy. So the
constraint is absolute: **no invented and no unattributed studies.**

## How it is enforced

The bibliography is not decorative. Each concept in the atlas carries its own
`citations` list; every Evidence Room exhibit and every Open Question that
asserts a fact references one of those entries by key. Those keys are
aggregated into a single registry, and any key referenced by an exhibit but
missing a real reference is surfaced as unresolved rather than quietly
rendered as though it were sound.

The consequence is that a claim cannot be displayed here without a source
attached to it. If a reference cannot be produced, the claim does not go up.

## What happens when there is no paper

It gets written down as unknown.

This is the part that separates this archive from a summary of neuroscience.
The absence of evidence is itself filed — as an exhibit, in the tier reserved
for it, in the same typeface as everything else. A gap is a finding. It is
recorded with the same care as a result, because for the questions this
investigation is actually asking, the gaps are most of the territory.
