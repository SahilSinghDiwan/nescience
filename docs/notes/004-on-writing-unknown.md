title: On Writing UNKNOWN
number: 004
summary: Why the word is set in red, given its own block, and never quietly omitted.

Every open question in this archive ends the same way: with a section stating
what remains unknown. It is always present. It is never the shortest section
by accident, and it is never dropped because the rest of the answer felt
substantial enough without it.

## Why it is marked

**UNKNOWN** is the one place the archive spends its single accent colour. That
is a deliberate allocation. In a monochrome archive, the only red on the page
marks the boundary of knowledge — which makes the boundary the most visible
thing in the room.

The alternative was to render the gaps in grey, the way uncertainty is usually
rendered: present, technically, but visually demoted below the confident
material. That treatment tells the reader the gaps are the leftovers. Here
they are the exhibit.

## Why an archive would rather not

There is a real pull, when writing a file, toward closure. A question with a
tidy answer feels finished. A question that ends in "nobody knows" feels like
the writer ran out of sources — and the format of an archive, with its case
numbers and stamps, makes unresolved entries look like incomplete work rather
than accurate ones.

That pressure is why the unknown section is structural rather than optional.
It cannot be omitted by a file that would rather end confidently, because the
format does not offer that ending.

## Nescience

The word means the state of not knowing — specifically, not knowing without
pretending otherwise. It is the subject of this archive, not a caveat attached
to it.

The point is not that the science is thin. In several of these files the
science is very good, and the tiers say so. The point is that the good science
runs out somewhere, and that somewhere is worth mapping precisely.
