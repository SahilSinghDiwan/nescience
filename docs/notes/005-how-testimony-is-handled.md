title: How Testimony Is Handled
number: 005
summary: What happens to a participant's answers, what is published, and what never is.

Participants give testimony by answering the interview. Because those answers
are personal, the handling rules are stated here in public rather than left
implicit in a privacy policy nobody opens.

## What is collected

A name, given up front, and answers to five modules: Experience · The Story ·
Decisions · The Unknown · Reflection. Nothing is pre-filled and nothing is
inferred — the record contains exactly what the participant typed.

## What is published

Only what the participant explicitly chose to publish, and only under a code.

At the end of the interview there is a direct question about whether the
responses should appear in the public archive. Publication is opt-in. A
participant who does not choose it is still on file for the investigation, but
appears nowhere public.

Published testimony is filed under a short code derived from the name — two
letters, numbered if that code is already taken. The code is what appears in
Witness Accounts. **Real names are never rendered on any public surface.**

This is enforced at the source rather than by template discipline. The public
surfaces are built from a projection that drops unpublished records and strips
private fields before the data reaches a template, so a public page has no
access to a real name even if it asked for one. There is a test whose only job
is to fail if that stops being true.

## What stays private

Real names, the name-to-code mapping, unpublished testimony, and timestamps.
These are visible only on the investigator surface, which is password-gated
and disabled outright when no password is configured — it cannot be left
accidentally open by default.

## Why testimony is in an evidence archive at all

The Witness Accounts are not illustrations decorating the research, and they
are not offered as evidence in the sense the Evidence Room uses that word. No
claim in a case file rests on them.

They are here because the questions this archive asks are about how experience
becomes identity, and the participants are the only people with direct access
to the inside of that process. Their answers are matched against the concept
atlas to surface leads — connections worth investigating — not conclusions.

Testimony indicates where to look. The papers decide what can be claimed.
